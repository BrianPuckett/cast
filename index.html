01 / The record

Some songs take a lifetime.

A debut album about time, distance, responsibility—and finding your way back to the thing you love.

Andy Koh began writing songs as a student in the 1980s. He spent the following decade performing and recording, then devoted his professional life to medicine. Music never quite disappeared. The ideas, melodies and unfinished songs remained, waiting for the right moment.

On October 30, 2026, those years converge in Taking the Long Way: nine songs that span different chapters of a life, newly realized with an exceptional group of musicians and engineers.

02 / The music

Nine chapters.
One journey.

From the early pulse of “The Test” to the title track that brings the story full circle, the record moves through doubt, hope, relationships and renewal. The songs were written across decades but belong together here.

“My Side” carries the memory of early musical collaborator Peter Wilson. “Taking the Long Way” became the title track after Andy could finally see the shape of the journey.

03 / The artist

The music never left.

Before he became a Dallas-based pediatric oncologist and researcher, Andy Koh was a young musician writing songs, playing shows and imagining a life onstage.

His musical journey began at Harvard in the 1980s and continued through the band Coloring Tunes in the 1990s. The band recorded and performed, attracting industry interest. Andy ultimately pursued medicine, but his connection to songwriting endured.

The new record brings together that early ambition with the perspective of decades spent building a family, practicing medicine and working in research. The result is not an attempt to relive the past; it is the work of an artist who has lived long enough to hear his own songs differently.

The long way wasn't a detour. It became the story.

04 / Behind the recording

Old songs.
New light.

Some material began decades ago; other songs were completed much later. The sessions brought Andy's long-held ideas to life with bassist Sean Hurley and drummer Jake Reed. The record was mixed by Grammy-winning engineer Darrell Thorp and mastered by Grammy-winning engineer Michael Romanowski.

The arrangements were made to serve the songs rather than overwhelm them. This is music shaped by persistence—and by the decision to finish what once seemed unfinished.
